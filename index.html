Why Statistical
Rigor Matters

Statistical significance can help assess whether observed data are compatible with a statistical null model, but it does not by itself establish that a finding is biologically meaningful, unbiased, or reproducible.

Our tools implement established calculation and analysis methods, but users should check each method’s assumptions, data quality, study design, and interpretation before drawing scientific conclusions.

Practical methodology

Choose a method before interpreting a result

Scientist's Toolkit brings common wet-lab calculations and introductory statistical workflows into one place. The result is only as reliable as the inputs, assumptions, and experimental design behind it. Use the method notes on each tool page to understand what is calculated and when a different analysis may be needed.

Preparing reagents

Use the molarity calculator when molecular weight, mass, volume, and concentration are known in compatible units. Use the dilution calculator for stock-to-working-solution calculations; check that the final volume and concentration match your protocol.

For hydrates, salts, purity corrections, or density-dependent preparations, verify the molecular form and assumptions separately.

Quantifying protein

The protein quantification tool uses a standard curve to estimate sample concentrations. Review the standard concentrations and the fitted relationship before accepting an estimate.

A high fit statistic does not by itself establish assay validity; inspect residuals, replicate variability, and whether samples fall within the calibrated range.

Comparing experimental groups

The t-test calculator supports independent and paired comparisons, while the chi-square calculator analyzes 2×2 categorical counts with Pearson and Fisher results. Choose the test based on the outcome and study design, not only on which result is significant.

Check independence, sample size, distributional assumptions, expected cell counts, and report effect sizes or confidence intervals where appropriate.

Reviewing unusual values and time-to-event data

The outlier detector can help flag observations for investigation; it should not be used to delete inconvenient values automatically. The survival analysis tool supports Kaplan–Meier workflows for time-to-event data.

Document exclusion criteria in advance and confirm censoring, event coding, and follow-up definitions before interpreting survival estimates.

Scope and scientific responsibility

These tools are intended for education and research support, not as a substitute for protocol review, statistical consultation, or clinical decision-making. Human-equivalent dose estimates are preliminary scaling calculations and do not establish a safe or effective human dose. Do not enter patient-identifiable or confidential unpublished data into public-facing tools.

The toolkit is developed by a researcher with a Ph.D. in Cancer Biology. Read the developer's background and project purpose, or consult the methodology and usage guide before applying a result to an experiment.
