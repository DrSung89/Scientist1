Method and assumptions

One-way ANOVA tests the null hypothesis that all group population means are equal. Standard ANOVA partitions total variation into between-group and within-group sums of squares; its F statistic is the ratio of their mean squares. Eta-squared (η²) reports the fraction of observed total variation associated with group membership in this one-factor sample.

Both methods assume independent observations. Standard ANOVA also assumes equal population variances; Welch ANOVA relaxes that assumption but still relies on an appropriate mean-based model. With small samples, severe skew or influential outliers can affect inference. A significant omnibus test indicates that not all means are equal; it does not identify which groups differ.

The pairwise section reports Welch comparisons with Bonferroni family-wise adjustment. It is a follow-up analysis and does not replace choosing contrasts in advance or reporting the omnibus result.

For two groups, the one-way ANOVA test is equivalent to a two-sided independent-samples pooled-variance t-test. For two independent groups with unequal variances, use the t-test calculator and select Welch's test.

For educational and research support. Choose the test based on the study design and assumptions; statistical significance alone does not establish practical importance.
